# Rough Terrain Comparison: step_argmin vs flat_10_30_50

## Setup

- **Terrain**: MuJoCo hfield, 1mm std (2mm mean) height, tiled 3x along +X
- **Resolution**: PIXELS_PER_SQUARE = 8 (8x8 pixels per 5mm logical tile)
- **Walls**: Transparent walls on both Y edges + back wall at terrain start to fully contain the robot on the hfield
- **Robot spawn**: 50mm forward (well onto hfield), 10mm above terrain surface
- **Jitter**: 5 trials per config with random Y offset (+/-5mm, clamped to hfield width minus 3mm body margin), best forward velocity selected
- **Sim duration**: 3s per trial
- **Morphologies**: scene1 (4-leg), scene2 (2-leg), scene4 (4-leg alt), scene_wheel (wheeled)
- **Frequencies**: 10, 30, 50 Hz

- Step params have 45x higher torsional/rolling friction

## Analysis

**step_argmin**: Extremely stable (zero tumble across all 12 configs), but almost completely stuck on 1mm rough terrain. Best forward velocity is 6.1 mm/s (wheel_f50). The high rotational friction and noslip solver keep the robot planted but unable to generate enough traction to overcome terrain features. Very low lateral displacement and yaw deviation -- the robot sits in place cleanly.

**flat_10_30_50**: Much higher velocities (mean 63.8 mm/s) but chaotic behavior -- 10/12 configs tumble, mean yaw deviation 86.2 deg. The scene2_f50 outlier (217.6 mm/s) and scene4_f30 (229.5 mm/s) are bouncing/tumbling their way forward, not controlled locomotion. The low friction params allow the robot to be flung over terrain features.

**scene_wheel**: Both param sets converge to similar low velocities (2-15 mm/s). Wheeled morphology is terrain-limited regardless of contact parameters. Notably, flat params show some tumbling on wheel at f30 (0.004) and f50 (0.019) that step params avoid entirely.

**scene4**: Interesting outlier for flat params -- scene4_f10 and scene4_f30 show decent velocity (34.6, 229.5 mm/s) with zero tumble and low yaw (<8 deg). This morphology may have a geometry that handles rough terrain better with low-friction params.

## Interpretation

1mm rough terrain features (~17% of robot body height) are effectively impassable for stable locomotion at this scale.

- **Step-optimized params** prioritize stability over mobility -- zero tumble but near-zero velocity
- **Flat-optimized params** achieve higher displacement through chaotic dynamics (tumbling, bouncing) rather than controlled walking
- Neither param set produces reliable, stable rough terrain locomotion
- The terrain is physically challenging: 1mm bumps on a ~6mm robot are equivalent to ~30cm obstacles for a 1.8m human

## Experimental Comparison

Real rough terrain data from physical experiments. Terrain generated by:
```
python terrain_mesh.py --nX 10 --nY 6 --sL 0.005 --height-mean 0.002 --height-std 0.001 --z-safe 0.00025 --seed 42 -o terrain --format obj
```
Same geometry spec as sim (1mm std, 2mm mean, 5mm tiles). Real terrain is 3D-printed OBJ prisms with sharp edges; sim uses hfield (smoothed heightmap).

Step_argmin is ~98% too slow (completely stuck). Flat params are closer (~52% mean abs error) but wildly inconsistent -- overshooting by 105% on some configs (tumbling artifacts) and -90%+ on wheel. The hfield contact model (smooth slopes) likely doesn't capture the grip that real prism edges provide.
